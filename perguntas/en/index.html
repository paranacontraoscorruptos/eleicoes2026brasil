Frequently asked questions

The most common questions about the statistical audit of Brazil's 2026 election tally sheets, the voting machine, the tally sheet, the data and privacy.

PT EN ES

About the site

What is this website?

It is an independent statistical analysis of the tally sheets (Boletins de Urna) of Brazil's 2026 General Elections, made only with public TSE data.

  • Every section, no sampling: 499,206 in round 1 and 499,207 in round 2;
  • 15 statistical and consistency tests;
  • reports in Portuguese, English and Spanish;
  • code and data to download, with a checksum (SHA-512).

Is this an official TSE website?

No. It is an independent analysis, with no link to the TSE. It uses only public data that the TSE itself publishes on its Open Data Portal.

Does the site say whether there was fraud?

No. The site draws no conclusions about the election result. It does not claim that there was, or that there was not, fraud, error or irregularity.

It publishes the data, the reports and the code so that each person, authority or party can make their own analysis. See what the analysis does not prove.

Do I need to pay or sign up?

No. Everything is free and there is no sign-up. The site can also be installed as an app on your phone, with the Install app button in the footer.

About the analysis

Which tests are run?

There are 15 tests by section, municipality or state: tally closing, totals, unanimity, zero abstention, blank and null votes, split voting, party vote, neighborhood, identical results, biometrics, times, machine replacement, 2nd digit, last digit and Klimek.

There is also the comparison of votes from round 1 to round 2. Each test is explained on the Tests page.

What does a “flagged section” mean?

It is a section that showed up in at least one test. There are two types:

  • statistical anomaly: an arithmetically impossible number or one that is very unlikely for the test's model;
  • informational: something unusual worth recording, but without the weight of an anomaly.

In round 2 there were 625 sections with an anomaly (0.13%); in round 1, 1,491 (0.30%). The full list is in Flagged sections.

Does a statistical anomaly prove anything?

No. It shows that a number is unlikely for the test's model, not the cause. It tells you what to check first.

The check is made with the printed tally sheet, the polling station minutes and the machine's signed files.

Why are some sections flagged?

With hundreds of thousands of sections tested, some flags appear by chance. To limit false alarms, the statistical tests use the Benjamini–Hochberg correction, with a false discovery rate of 0.01.

The site does not judge whether the number of flagged sections is high or low.

What does the analysis not do?

  • It does not examine each machine's signed files (.bu, .rdv, .logjez);
  • it does not show the cause of a flag;
  • it does not compare with previous elections or use the first-digit Benford test;
  • it does not prove that the machine recorded what the voter typed.

All of this is in Limitations.

Where does the data come from?

From the TSE Open Data Portal (dadosabertos.tse.jus.br):

  • 2026 tally sheets (files by state and round);
  • expected and actual correspondences (used in test 12);
  • the 2026 polling place register (name and address of each place).

Each TSE file has an official checksum next to it. The data was used without changing its content.

Can I redo the analysis myself?

Yes. The code is open and runs on your computer. The How to replicate page shows the steps and how to check that the result matches the published one.

Voting machine and tally sheet

What is the tally sheet (boletim de urna)?

It is the paper the machine prints when voting ends, with that section's result: how many could vote, how many voted and each candidate's votes. A copy is posted on the section's door and the same content is published by the TSE.

See each part explained in Tally sheet anatomy.

What is the tally sheet's QR code for?

The QR code holds the tally sheet's numbers in a format the phone can read. On the Check a printed tally page you read the QR code with the camera and the site compares it, number by number, with the file published by the TSE. The image stays on your device.

Why did the machine open at 6 or 7 a.m.?

The tally sheet times are in local time. Voting runs from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Brasília time:

  • Acre: 6 a.m. to 3 p.m. local;
  • Amazonas (most of it), Rondônia, Roraima, Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul: 7 a.m. to 4 p.m. local;
  • Abroad: 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. in the country's time.

Test 11 and the time charts convert each municipality to the right time zone.

I could not find my section. What now?

Check the state and the zone (they are on your voter card or in the e-Título app). If your section was merged into another one, its votes appear under the main section number; the lookup shows the “Aggregated sections” field.

How do I see the sections that vote at the same place as me?

On the Polling places page you choose the state and municipality (or use Near me) and open the place. All its sections appear side by side, with votes, turnout and times. There are 94,302 places across the country.

How to use

How do I see my section's result?

On the Your section page, choose the state and type the zone and section (they are on your voter card or in the e-Título app). You will see the votes in both rounds, the machine times and whether the section showed up in any test.

Can I search by name, CPF or voter card number?

No. The vote is secret and the tally sheet has no voter data. Lookups are only by state, municipality, zone, section and polling place.

What is Atlas?

It is the site's question area. You type or say your question and it answers with the site's data, showing where each piece of information came from. It runs in your browser: what you type is not sent anywhere. Open Atlas.

How do I download the data?

  • Downloads: reports, spreadsheets, code and the full package;
  • For developers: the site's data files, explained field by field;
  • Advanced query: filter and combine the data in the browser and download the result.

Can I use the numbers in a news story or post?

Yes, citing the source and the site's link. The Press kit has the main numbers, ready-made texts, images and the right way to cite. Say “statistical anomaly”, never “fraud”.

I found an error. How do I report it?

Use the Report an error button (in the footer and on each section you look up). It opens a text that already includes the page; describe the problem and send it via GitHub or paste it in the Telegram group. Do not include personal data.

Data, proofs and privacy

How do I know the files were not changed later?

In three ways:

  • each file has a SHA-512 code you can check in Check a file;
  • the reports and data have a proof of date recorded on Bitcoin (OpenTimestamps);
  • there are copies of the site saved on the Internet Archive.

It is all explained in Proofs and copies.

Does the site keep my data?

The site counts visits with GoatCounter, without cookies and without identifying you. What you type in Atlas, saved sections, queries and preferences stay only on your device. The Near me location does not leave your device either.

Can I put a chart from this site on my website or blog?

Yes. The Embed page builds the code to place a section, a chart, the map or the main numbers on any website, with the source already shown.
